# The Quiet Rules - 3 CLIPS per reel (~18-20 s). Topics Muse ke, lines lambi.
Har reel: Clip A (hook) -> Clip B (beech ka detail) -> Clip C (Rule). Har clip ~14 lafz, 8 s ke andar.
Flow lock: reels/adrian-series/quiet-rules-week1.md wala LOW-ENERGY lock (spotlight look) har clip mein.

1 Overthinking
A: "You rewrote that message ten times, then deleted it and said nothing at all."
B: "You told yourself you'd send it when the words felt perfect. They never did."
C: "Rule one: say it badly. Badly said still beats never said. Send it."

2 People-pleasing
A: "You said yes to dinner when your whole body wanted to stay home. Again."
B: "You smiled, you showed up, and you were gone before the food arrived."
C: "Rule two: a tired yes helps no one. Rest is not rude. Rest is honest."

3 Comparison
A: "You scrolled past his win and suddenly your own life felt small."
B: "He posted the result. You're comparing it to your Tuesday afternoon, the unfinished version."
C: "Rule three: his chapter ten is not your chapter three. Keep reading yours."

4 Too much advice
A: "You asked five people what to do, and now you're more lost than before."
B: "Five answers, five different lives, and none of them were standing in yours."
C: "Rule four: your gut already answered. Stop polling the room. Trust the first one."

5 Replaying conversations
A: "It's midnight and you're still replaying what you said at lunch, word by word."
B: "You've fixed that sentence in your head six times. Nobody else is thinking about it."
C: "Rule five: the conversation ended hours ago. You're the only one still in it."

6 Staying silent
A: "You had the answer in the meeting but let someone else say it first."
B: "Then they said it a little worse than you would have, and got the credit."
C: "Rule six: speak before you're sure. Certainty comes after the sentence, not before it."

7 Starting before you feel ready
A: "You've been waiting to feel ready for almost a year now."
B: "Ready kept moving. Every time you got close, you decided you needed one more month."
C: "Rule seven: ready is a feeling, not a requirement. Start small today. Start badly."
