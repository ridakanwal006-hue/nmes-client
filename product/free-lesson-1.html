The Voice Isn't a Fact

A 5-minute lesson on the first thing you can do about self-doubt.

Self-doubt sounds like the truth for one simple reason: it's the voice you have listened to the longest. It says "you're not ready", "they'll think it's stupid", "everyone else has it figured out", and because you've heard it for years, it feels like a fact.

It isn't. It's a habit of thought. And a habit can be tested.

Today's goal is not to silence the voice.
It's to stop letting it make your decisions. Doubt can ride along. It just doesn't get the wheel.
1

Catch it (2 min)

Write down 3 doubts you told yourself this week, word for word, the way the voice actually says them.

2

Test it (2 min)

Next to each one, mark it: F = a Fact I can prove, R = a Fear about what might happen, H = an old Habit of thought. Most doubts turn out to be fears and habits, not facts.

3

Rewrite it (1 min)

Turn each one into: "I notice I'm thinking ______. What is one small step I can take anyway?"

YOUR FIRST REP

One small step today

Self-trust is built the same way a muscle is: with small reps, not big motivation. Pick one of these and do it in the next 5 minutes:

  • Send the message you keep rewriting, in its rough version.
  • Say one opinion out loud that you would normally swallow.
  • Make one small decision without asking anybody.

It does not have to go well. It only has to happen. That is the rep.
