# Men 40+ pain points on YouTube, TikTok and Instagram (video content and comments)

Method and caveats. Data pulled 2026-10-10 with vidIQ MCP tools: `vidiq_instagram_tiktok_outlier_search` (IG/TT, window from 2026-04-10, English, 2 calls), `vidiq_ig_accounts_from_outliers`, `vidiq_ig_profile_reels`, `vidiq_youtube_search` (sorted by views, window from 2026-04-10), `vidiq_outliers`, `vidiq_video_comments`, `vidiq_comment_insights` (discover, sampled). Tool outputs are labelled "[vidIQ ...]" and carry no public URL unless shown. Reel/video links are built from the IDs the tools returned. Instagram and TikTok comments could NOT be read (the tools only expose YouTube comments), so comment evidence is from YouTube only. The WebSearch tool returned nothing useful on creator products. Outlier multiples are vidIQ's "x creator median". Demographic labels (e.g. "men 40-60") are vidIQ's AI-inferred audience guesses, not measured.

## 1. Which videos about men over 40 have outlier views, and what hook and pain point made them work?

### Takeaway
The biggest reactions cluster around five pains: (1) divorce and the empty house, (2) male loneliness and "I'm done with relationships / I choose peace", (3) dating after 40/50 and women "who dumped their husbands", (4) job loss, age discrimination and starting over broke in the 50s, (5) retirement dread and "years left". Winning hooks are a first-person confession with an age and a number ("I'm 55 and for the first time I can't find a job", "49-year-old divorced dad, these 6 things hit harder than I expected") or a blunt reframe ("Being single isn't the problem. Hating your own life is"). Almost all are static talking-head or text-over-B-roll under 2 minutes.

### Inferences
- The strongest pattern across platforms: a numbered list of what the creator learned the hard way, with a stated age, over a still image (listicle-confession). It outperformed creators' medians by 20x to 470x.
- The hooks that work promise permission or relief ("being single is not the problem", "a place called peace") rather than a fix. On YouTube the engagement is a gathering of men telling their own stories rather than advice-taking.
- Women creators are getting some of the largest numbers in this space (@frigidbarrell 1.8M, @rachaelsloancoach, @new.man.codes presenter), by talking about or to men over 40. A woman reacting to a divorced man's video was the single biggest TikTok result I saw.
- Retirement is a bigger aggregate view pool than divorce on TikTok, but those audiences are gender-neutral and mostly positive in tone.

### Gaps
- Not found: any qualifying results for bald-man dating, widowers, fatherhood of adult kids, ageing parents or ED confidence as male-40+ topics at outlier scale. My IG/TikTok queries mixed topics, so the top results were dominated by divorce and retirement; separate narrow queries were not run (credit and call budget). Only the widower mention appears in the Forged in Silence description and in two YouTube comments.
- No vidIQ call returned a post date for individual TikTok videos, so "last 6 months" rests on the search date filter (from 2026-04-10).
- @new.man.codes presenter gender comes from a vidIQ visual note and the account's "Kings of Compassion" branding says it targets men; I did not verify who presents.
- The Instagram "view" counts for pinned reels (e.g. mikeradoor 2.7M) are plays, not necessarily views; the outlier tool reports "views".

## 2. What do comments on those posts say (what viewers confess, ask for, argue about)?

### Takeaway
On YouTube, the top-liked comments from men 40-60 are not requests for help; they are statements of contentment-in-isolation ("addicted to peace and quiet", "never living with someone again") mixed with grievance about marriage, family courts and child support, and a minority of confessions of empty-house loneliness, being cheated on, and being stuck in a dead marriage planning an exit. Direct requests are for community (meetups, men's seminars, other men's stories) and for "what comes next" after divorce. Instagram and TikTok comments could not be retrieved.

- The strongest unmet ask in sampled comments is for male community and peer stories, not advice.
- Comment sections on male-audience YouTube channels skew toward anti-marriage/anti-women views; this may not represent men who follow coaching reels on IG.

### Gaps
- No Instagram or TikTok comment text (tool limitation). I used no scraping alternatives.
- YouTube comments were sampled from 3 videos only; no comments pulled on job loss/age-discrimination, retirement, bald/looks, ED or ageing-parent videos, so those pains' comment themes are unknown.
- Commenter ages are mostly self-stated; I did not verify any.

## 4. Pain points in comments that creators do not address (gaps)

### Takeaway
Creators mostly offer "peace and rebuilding" reframes, but commenters raise concrete practical problems that none of the videos I read address: how to exit a dead but "peaceful" marriage and untangle money and the house, child support and custody fairness, fear of dying alone versus enjoying solitude, health and sex without a partner, and where to meet other men.

### Cited Findings
- Exit logistics: a 47-year-old engineer asks "How did you end it brother?" and says he has tried for nearly a year to have the talk and dreads separating finances and the house (replies under the top comments on Forged in Silence; 5 and 2 likes). The video itself is about living alone, not how to leave. — [vidIQ comments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WObCsQA93J4)
- Child support and custody: commenter paying since his early 20s (16 likes) and "Family law is unfair... men lose the kids" (50 likes, from vidIQ comment insights). Hooks reviewed cover emotional healing; none of the sampled hooks addresses legal or financial exit. — [vidIQ comment insights](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qJStK3_Culo)
- Next chapter after divorce: "what about the next chapter?... Life 2.0" request (4 likes). — [vidIQ comment insights](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2SrPQxZMkEI)
- Community and in-person: requests for men's seminars and meet-ups (4 and 7 likes). — [vidIQ comment insights](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=noe27Si2vOQ)
- Dating-app effort and conversation (7 and 4 likes, comments by men aged around 37). — [vidIQ comment insights](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rh2xNOQCMUg)
- Dying alone: "don't die alone bro it sucks" argued against by "we all die alone in the end" on Forged in Silence. — [vidIQ comments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WObCsQA93J4)
- Single fathers: a 58-year-old shift-working single dad says women show "zero interest" and he is called incel (4 likes). — [vidIQ comments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gn0UBOYDlfE)
- Widowers: two commenters (68 and a man widowed "last year") say they will not date again (11 and 7 likes), while no sampled outlier video was addressed to widowers specifically. — [vidIQ comments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gn0UBOYDlfE)
- Thinking about betrayal: men who were cheated on 20 years ago or 1.5 years ago say they think about it daily (184 and 17 likes); the creators' replies suggest "try one more time", which commenters did not take up. — [vidIQ comments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WObCsQA93J4)

### Inferences
- Likely content gaps: (1) "how to leave a roommate marriage" with money and house logistics; (2) cheating trauma that lingers decades; (3) post-divorce legal-financial education for men (child support age limits, custody); (4) widower-specific and single-dad dating content; (5) in-person men's groups/meetups; (6) ageing-parent caregiving and health or sexual confidence (no comments on these were found, so these are absences in my sample, not demonstrated unmet demand).

### Gaps
- Ageing-parent, bald/dating, ED/sex, job-loss and retirement-dread comment sections were not read at all.
- All gap claims come from 3-4 YouTube comment sections plus a sampled 600-comment insight run; low counts in the discover run (1-2 threads per theme) mean none of them is validated demand.
