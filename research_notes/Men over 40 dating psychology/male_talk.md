# How men (especially 40+) talk to each other: friendship, banter, locker-room talk, what is said and hidden

Method note: WebSearch worked (results are search-engine summaries); WebFetch failed with DNS errors (getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND) on the two attempts (RMIT, NCBI/PMC), so NO full text was read. Everything below rests on abstracts, snippets and secondary coverage. Evidence labels: STRONG = large/representative or peer-reviewed with clear finding; MEDIUM = peer-reviewed/qualitative or thesis, small samples; ANECDOTAL = popular press, blogs, podcasts, commentary. Most academic work found is on young men or all ages; very little is specific to 40+. No Reddit/forum posts were retrieved, so no forum quotes are given (none invented).

## 1. What does research say about male friendship patterns and communication (Way, Wright, Tannen, Bird, Swain, humour/teasing)?

### Takeaway
Male friendship is typically built around shared activity and "commonality" rather than disclosure; closeness is expressed covertly through doing things together, humour and teasing, while explicit emotional talk is rarer. Whether this is natural preference or learned suppression is contested (Way argues boys start out emotionally intimate and lose it under masculinity pressure; Tannen's "report vs rapport" framing is itself challenged).

### Cited Findings
- Way (Harvard UP, 2011): hundreds of interviews with black, Latino, white and Asian American boys through adolescence; boys describe intense, intimate friendships (say they'd go "wacko" without their best friend) that they lose as they become men, becoming distrustful and isolated; Way blames stereotypes that frame vulnerability and intimacy as feminine or gay and calls it a "crisis of connection". Evidence about adolescents, not 40+. MEDIUM (qualitative, large interview base) — [Foyles/publisher summary](https://www.foyles.co.uk/book/deep-secrets/niobe-way/9780674046641); [Times Higher Education review notes Way challenges Tannen's 1990 account of boys' peer groups](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/80825)
- Paul H. Wright (1982, Sex Roles): male friends tended to emphasise commonality (shared activities/experiences) while female friends emphasised reciprocity/face-to-face sharing; among very strong, long-lasting friendships no appreciable sex differences were found. MEDIUM (old study, snippet only; I could not open the PDF) — [Wright 1982 PDF (via search result)](https://isidore.co/misc/Physics%20papers%20and%20books/Zotero/storage/7TRHBMK9/Wright%20-%201982%20-%20Men's%20friendships,%20women's%20friendships%20and%20the%20all.pdf)
- The "face to face vs side by side" image is traced in one search summary to C.S. Lewis (lovers face each other, friends stand side by side facing a shared interest); side-by-side interaction (gaming, bar outings, watching sport) described as extremely common among men. ANECDOTAL — [InsideHook essay](https://www.insidehook.com/mental-health/men-side-side-interaction/amp)
- Tannen's "You Just Don't Understand" (1990): women use "rapport-talk" (affiliation), men "report-talk" (information); the book was regarded with controversy by academics. A small UCLA-hosted study (72 survey participants, 3 observational studies) found men's report talk is assertive and centred on expertise and facts. MEDIUM/weak for the UCLA study — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Just_Don%27t_Understand); [UCLA Languaged Life](https://languagedlife.ucla.edu/tag/gossip/)
- Swain: coined "closeness in the doing" (intimacy achieved by doing things with friends); male affection called "covert", with razzing and backslapping read as indirect signs of intimacy. ANECDOTAL as relayed (Psychology Today article; I did not retrieve Swain's primary text) — [Psychology Today, "Men, Women, and Friendship"](https://www.psychologytoday.com/articles/200611/men-women-and-friendship)
- Bird (1996, Gender & Society 10(2):120-132): men's same-sex (homosocial) interaction maintains hegemonic masculinity — emotional detachment, competitiveness, treating women as sexual objects — as the norm men are held accountable to even when they privately disagree; alternative masculinities are confined to mixed-gender settings or suppressed. STRONG as an influential theory (abstract read only; theory, not measured prevalence) — [UNC listing](https://gwonline.unc.edu/node/2256); [full-text PDF link (not opened)](https://bristol.ac.uk/media-library/sites/law/documents/Bird%20-%20Welcome%20to%20the%20Men's%20Club.pdf)
- Teasing/insults: a qualitative study of 7 young rural Victorian (Australia) men examined the role of insults in close male friendships (RMIT; abstract only). MEDIUM, young men — [RMIT repository](https://research-repository.rmit.edu.au/articles/journal_contribution/_That_whole_macho_male_persona_thing_The_role_of_insults_in_young_australian_male_friendships/27501678)
- A Facebook corpus thesis (1,500 friend interactions) found men about eight times more likely to make insulting/degrading jokes to other men than women were to each other (thesis, not peer reviewed; measures joking, not disclosure). A Griffith University conversation analyst reports close friends tease, mock and insult to show friendship, and that being able to receive it matters more than giving it (small qualitative data). MEDIUM/weak — [Griffith News](https://news.griffith.edu.au/?p=37694); [search-result list incl. the thesis](https://ir.lib.uwo.ca/etd/1336/)
- Costs of banter: Barbara Plester (Auckland) notes jocular abuse can slide into bullying and targets may fear exclusion if they complain (her suspicion, not a finding). ANECDOTAL/expert comment — [Griffith/commentary result](https://news.griffith.edu.au/?p=37694)
- Ridge & Broom (2023, The Conversation/Daily Maverick): male friendships are often portrayed as shallow, but humour, resistance to masculinity norms and bromances carry closeness without verbal expression. Young men. MEDIUM (summary only) — [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-05-22-he-is-always-there-to-listen-friendships-between-young-men-are-more-than-just-beers-and-banter.md)
- Bromance research (2017, Men and Masculinities, 30 undergraduates): men felt less judged and found it easier to resolve conflict and express emotion in bromances than romantic relationships; Winchester work reports non-sexual touch/cuddling. Young men, mostly UK/US students. MEDIUM — [Time](https://time.com/4978727/bromance-male-friendships/); [Winchester](https://winchester.ac.uk/News-and-Events/Press-Centre/Media-Articles/Study-shows-men-get-more-from-bromances-than-heterosexual-relationships-.php)
- Thomas Yarrow (via podcast blog): many men prefer camaraderie around shared activities to deep disclosure. ANECDOTAL — [podcast blog](https://podcast-insider.on-forge.com/blog/today-in-focus/the-men-trying-to-do-friendship-better-podcast)
- Friend counts: Survey Center on American Life, May 2021 (n=2,019 US adults): men with 6+ close friends fell from 55% (1990) to 27%; men with none rose from 3% to 15% (21% of single men vs 12% of married); only 10% of women have none; men feel less emotionally connected to friends. All ages. STRONG (survey), figures via secondary coverage — [National Review summary](https://www.nationalreview.com/2021/07/american-men-suffer-a-friendship-recession/amp/); [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/lifestyle/american-men-friendship-recession-report.amp)
- UK Movember 2018: 27% of men said no close friends; 22% of men 55+ never see friends; friendships weaken with age. MEDIUM (secondary report) — [inkl article](https://www.inkl.com/news/we-don-t-do-deep-emotional-discussions-why-men-lose-their-friends-and-how-they-can-make-more)
- Dunbar (quoted): close friends and family numbers are among the strongest predictors of health and lifespan. ANECDOTAL as relayed — same inkl article.

### Inferences
- The consistent pattern across sources: intimacy is expressed through activity, presence, teasing and humour, not stated feelings; an outsider reading banter as hostility or coldness would misread it (inference from Griffith, Swain, Ridge & Broom).
- Way's account implies the reticence is partly social enforcement (Bird's "accountable to the norm") rather than inability; the 40+ man may be quietly missing intimacy he once had.

### Gaps
- Could not open Wright 1982, Swain, Way, Bird full texts. Wright's "side-by-side" vs "face-to-face" wording is not confirmed verbatim in sources I saw. Little research specific to men 40+; I found only an untested Korean dissertation on middle-aged men's empathetic small-group conversation ([Ewha repository](https://dspace.ewha.ac.kr/handle/2015.oak/248715)) and a Goldsmiths thesis on Chinese men's friendship talk ([Goldsmiths](https://research.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/33666)), neither read.

## 2. What is "locker-room talk": function, content, performance, change after 40

### Takeaway
Research frames it as homosocial bonding and status-testing in which men are accountable to a shared masculine norm; content ranges widely and only a minority is degrading. How much is performance is not measured by anything I found; age effects are barely studied.

### Cited Findings
- Norwegian night-time-economy study (Vaynman, Sandberg & Pedersen, 2019; 100+ men): most sex stories were upbeat and erotically charged; a minority aggressive and degrading, which authors say serve male bonding, making sense of confusing experiences and excusing aggression. MEDIUM-STRONG (peer reviewed; mainly younger men, drinking contexts) — [search-result summary](https://repository.unpad.ac.id/handle/kandaga/210110190109) (secondary listing; original not opened)
- Curry (1991) fieldwork on two college sports teams: teammates faced put-downs so they kept to talk affirming traditional masculinity, treating women as objects; sexual conquest talk is a bonding ritual and abstainers can feel excluded. MEDIUM, young athletes — [commentary via search](https://synapsesocial.com/papers/W110751344)
- Experimental thesis (UNBC, 2018): hearing degrading or respectful locker-room talk; main hypothesis unsupported, but higher-sexism men awarded masculine status to men who talked about sexual encounters regardless of tone. MEDIUM-weak (master's thesis) — [UNBC](https://unbc.arcabc.ca/dissertations-and-theses/19889)
- Counterpoint: not every locker room talks that way; subjects include traffic, financial portfolios, athletic performance claims (student essay). ANECDOTAL — [NTU essay](https://oss.adm.ntu.edu.sg/vkoh006)
- NBC/WSJ poll after the 2016 Trump tape: biggest attitude disparities were by generation and education (breakdown not seen). MEDIUM — [NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/card/locker-room-talk-numbers-n665886)
- Historian notes men discussing women appear in medieval sources, i.e. not new. ANECDOTAL — [History Workshop](https://www.historyworkshop.org.uk/locker-room-talk-men-discussing-women-in-the-middle-ages-and-now/)

### Inferences
- Bird's accountability mechanism plus the UNBC result suggest sexual bragging is partly a status currency and partly performance for the audience; bravado is likely larger than behaviour, but no source measured exaggeration.
- Plausibly, 40+ men talk less about conquest and more about health, money, work and family, but this is my inference, not cited.

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence on how much sexual talk is exaggeration, or how locker-room talk changes after 40. Gary McNair's recorded-men theatre project exists but is not research.

## 3. Settings: pubs, sports, gym, sheds, workplace, golf, fishing, hobby clubs, group chats, forums

### Takeaway
Only Men's Sheds were found with real research; they support the idea that activity-centred, humour-rich settings make sensitive talk possible indirectly. Everything else (topics, slang, in-jokes, WhatsApp, Reddit) is a gap in what I could source.

### Cited Findings
- Men's Sheds, Kelly et al. 2021 (BMC Public Health; 62 members across five Sheds, interviews): Sheds act as an informal, male-friendly route into health engagement, with effects that often operate indirectly. MEDIUM — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7981817); [QUB](https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/publications/mens-sheds-as-an-alternative-healthcare-route-a-qualitative-study/)
- Sheds' slogan "shoulder to shoulder" originated from a 2008 Australian conference line about men preferring side-by-side to face-to-face; Irish Sheds for Life evaluation: share of men comfortable talking about mental health up by 20%, physical-activity guideline compliance 31.2% to 51.5%; members reported more meaningful health conversations. MEDIUM (programme evaluation, self-report) — [Men's Sheds Ireland](https://menssheds.ie/sheds-for-life-impact-report-launched/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men%27s_shed)
- 2022 systematic review (Am J Men's Health): benefits reported but evidence limited; conceptual review: small samples, self-report, little proof of physical-health effects. STRONG caution — [PMC conceptual review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6772158/); [PMC table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8928410/table/table5-15579883221084490)
- Steven Markham (via blog): Shed men shared in a friendly setting and communicated with humour non-judgmentally. ANECDOTAL (secondary) — [Shedworking blog](https://www.shedworking.co.uk/2016/09/mens-sheds-make-men-healthier-new.html?m=0)
- 2024 Health Promotion International commentary (via search summary): supportive relationships do not form automatically when men gather; coaching toward mutual help needed. MEDIUM — [Cardiff Met listing](https://pure.cardiffmet.ac.uk/en/publications/mens-help-seeking-for-distress-navigating-varied-pathways-and-pra/) (related item; the commentary itself not opened)

### Inferences
- Pubs, sport, gym, golf, fishing and DIY clubs function like sheds: a shared object gives men a reason to be together and a buffer for talk (my inference from the shed evidence).

### Gaps
- No sourced typical topics, slang, in-jokes for pubs/barbershops/golf/gym; no WhatsApp/Telegram/Reddit data or quotes retrieved (Reddit searches returned articles only).

## 4. Advice-seeking and responses when a man opens up

### Takeaway
Men often ask indirectly (framing, humour, practical problems) and respond with practical help or deflecting humour; peer help-seeking is shaped by friends and family and often reframed as consistent with masculine values. Direct tests of "asking for a friend" in real male peer talk were not found.

### Cited Findings
- UK qualitative study (38 men): family and friendship networks strongly shape help-seeking; some men reconstruct help-seeking to align with masculine values despite ambivalence. MEDIUM — [Frontiers in Sociology, 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8661038/)
- APS 2022 poster: vignette where a person asked for help directly vs indirectly ("for a friend") changed ratings of the friend's familiarity, perceived severity and helpfulness. Conference abstract, MEDIUM-weak — [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/conventions/archive/2022annual/paper/40626/)
- Erentzen, Quinlan & Mar: humour in men's help-seeking campaigns aims to avoid defensiveness (summary ambiguous). MEDIUM-weak — [Care for Your Mind](https://careforyourmind.org/two-guys-walk-into-a-therapists-office/)
- 2026 Eastern Europe/South Caucasus report: men prefer trusted relationships, anonymous digital platforms and indirect, practical, autonomy-respecting support. MEDIUM (different region) — [MFJ](https://mfj.se/en/articles/2026/06/new-study-men-in-eastern-europe-seek-support-but-are-limited-by-masculinity-norms)
- Divorced Vancouver comedian (CBC): didn't call male friends when lonely — "As a guy, you don't do that"; friendships had lapsed after marriage/kids; a writer says hiking friends' talk skips marriages entirely. ANECDOTAL — [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.4659325); [Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/opinion/article-why-do-men-have-such-a-difficult-time-maintaining-friendships)

### Inferences
- Likely patterns (uncited): a man tests the water with a joke or hypothetical; if the listener laughs it off, the topic is dropped without loss of face, and if listener lingers, it opens. Sources support indirectness, not this sequence.

### Gaps
- No study found on how male peers actually respond to a man opening up, or on divorce/health/money/sex talk specifics for 40+. The divorce statistics on legal-advice sites (50% less likely therapy, 39% suicide) were unverified and are excluded. Dutch data: divorced people's emotional loneliness relates to missing a partner, social loneliness to network deficits (Dykstra & Fokkema 2007, all genders) — [NIDI PDF](https://www.nidi.nl/shared/content/output/2007/basp-29-01-dykstra.pdf).

## 5. Talk about wives, ex-wives and dating in male-only spaces; manosphere vs ordinary men

### Takeaway
I found no sourced description of how ordinary men 40+ talk about wives, exes and dating. What is sourced: the online "manosphere" is studied separately, its adherents are self-selected, and links are correlational. Do not treat manosphere tropes as typical.

### Cited Findings
- Two correlational studies (N=311, N=470): men less aware of their privilege feel more threatened by feminists, which goes with endorsing manosphere attitudes; those recognising privilege show lower affinity. Not causal. MEDIUM — [PMC12011899](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12011899/)
- Ofcom qualitative study of manosphere engagement: sample self-selecting, extreme views possibly under-reported. MEDIUM — [Ofcom PDF](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/online-safety/research-statistics-and-data/protecting-children/experiences-of-engaging-with-the-manosphere.pdf)
- A UK government manosphere report is disputed: critics say its ambiguity on misogyny is misleading (partisan blog; contested) — [We Hunted the Mammoth](https://www.wehuntedthemammoth.com/2025/06/21/the-guardian-runs-interference-for-a-shoddy-terrible-uk-government-report-on-the-manosphere/)
- Equimundo Man Box (2017; men 18-30, US/UK/Mexico): most young men feel pressure to conform to rigid norms (self-sufficiency, toughness, sexual prowess); high-conformity men fare worse; young men often turn to online spaces. Young men only. STRONG for young men — [Equimundo](https://www.equimundo.org/resources/man-box/)
- Advocacy factsheet citing UN Women: younger men more likely than older men to hold regressive gender-role ideas; 75% of Australian young men engaging with masculinity influencers say women have it easier. ADVOCACY, low weight — [IWDA factsheet](https://iwda.org.au/assets/files/Manosphere-Factsheet-16-days-of-activism.pdf)
- Facebook insult-joke thesis and sports-team studies above show degrading talk exists among men, mostly young, mostly in bonding contexts (see section 2).

### Inferences
- Bitterness about ex-wives among divorced 40+ men probably appears as complaint about money, custody and "getting taken", but I have no source; treat as hypothesis. Forum content is selected for grievance and not representative.

### Gaps
- No sourced quotes from Reddit/forums (e.g., divorced-dads or "men over 40 dating" communities); no research on 40+ men's private talk about wives or dating; no representative comparison of ordinary men vs manosphere. A podcast series on divorce recovery for men over 40 exists ([DPTSP](https://podcast.ausha.co/divorce-recovery-men-over-40-podcast/how-to-date-after-divorce-at-40-real-talk-for-men-special-guest-dr-hana-gala-dptsp-131)) but is not research and was not reviewed.

## Not sourced (what men "never say out loud")
Nothing here beyond the sections above: fear, loneliness, sexual performance, money stress were not documented from primary evidence. The closest cited signals are the CBC divorced-man quote and Way's account of lost intimacy.
