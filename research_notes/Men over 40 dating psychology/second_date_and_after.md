# Second date and early relationship stages for people 40+ (men 40+ focus)

Method note: WebFetch was tried once (PMC article) and failed with a DNS error (ENOTFOUND), so every finding below comes from WebSearch result summaries, not full texts. Strength tags: [strong] = peer-reviewed or large national survey; [medium] = smaller or older study, or reputable secondary summary; [anecdotal] = advice column, commentary, forum-style. This is a thin evidence area: there is very little age-specific research on the second date itself. Nothing below is advice to manipulate anyone.

## 1. How relationships progress after 40/50: repartnering, cohabitation, LAT, divorced vs widowed

### Takeaway
Among people who divorce at 50+, men repartner far more often than women, mostly via cohabitation rather than remarriage. Divorced people repartner more than widowed people (a gap that holds for women but not men). Living apart together (LAT) is a real, motivated choice for independence and finances, but US research on it is limited.

### Cited Findings
- [strong] HRS analysis (1998-2014): about 22% of women and 37% of men repartnered within 10 years after gray divorce, more often by cohabitation than remarriage, particularly for men. Economic resources, health and social ties predicted repartnering — [PMC6450723 / HRS abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6450723); see also [Newswise summary](https://www.newswise.com/articles/marital-separation-reconciliation-and-repartnering-in-later-life)
- [strong] NCFMR Family Profile (2025): older cohabiting adults reached 4.6 million by 2022, up from under 1 million in 2000; many older adults choose cohabitation over remarriage — [BGSU NCFMR FP-25-08](https://www.bgsu.edu/ncfmr/resources/data/family-profiles/FP-25-08.html)
- [medium] Receipt of Social Security discouraged entry into remarriage vs staying unpartnered (Wisconsin research brief, financial incentive angle) — [RDRC brief](https://rdrc.wisc.edu/files/research-briefs/Wi23-q2_Research-Brief_6.28.24_Brown.pdf)
- [strong] HRS 50+: divorced people were more often repartnered than widowed people; the gap persisted for women after controls but disappeared for men — [PMC6093496](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6093496)
- [medium] Missouri interviews with adults 60+ in LAT relationships: motives were staying independent, keeping their own homes, preserving family boundaries and financial independence; challenges include caregiving and decision-making — [Missouri news release](https://munewsarchives.missouri.edu/news-releases/2017/0209-older-adults-embracing-living-apart-together/index.html); [Next Avenue](https://www.nextavenue.org/older-adults-embrace-living-apart-together/)
- [medium, poster, preliminary] BGSU poster comparing single, dating, LAT, cohabiting, remarried older adults; notes US LAT research is limited — [Wu PAA 2018](https://www.bgsu.edu/content/dam/BGSU/college-of-arts-and-sciences/NCFMR/documents/PAA/wu-huijing-paa-2018.pdf)
- [medium] ~40% of middle-aged/older couples with children are in stepfamilies; cohabiting stepfamilies most disadvantaged; partner relationship quality largely similar across married families, married stepfamilies and cohabiting stepfamilies (Journals of Gerontology B 2018, abstract only) — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/geronb/v73y2018i6p1043-1054..html)

### Inferences
- Men 40+ who have divorced are statistically more likely than women to end up partnered again, and often in cohabitation, so "when do we live together?" may come before "do we marry?".
- Typical timelines to exclusivity, sex or moving in for 40+ were not found (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No sourced figures on median time to exclusivity, sex, or moving in for 40+ daters. No US study directly comparing LAT among widowed vs divorced people. Age 40-49 is poorly covered; most research is 50+/60+.

## 2. What men 40+ and women 40+ want and fear

### Takeaway
Evidence is mostly qualitative and from widowhood studies. Men tend to repartner to relieve loneliness and for care/companionship; women more often weigh the loss of independence. I found no research framing "fear of commitment" as such.

### Cited Findings
- [medium] UK thesis (25 widows, 26 widowers): core gender difference is perceived cost/benefit of a new partner — widows saw it as at the cost of their new independence; widowers gained relief from loneliness — [Surrey thesis](https://openresearch.surrey.ac.uk/esploro/outputs/doctoral/Gender-age-and-widowhood-How-older/99513934402346)
- [medium] Israeli study of 27 couples who both repartnered after widowhood: men tended to experience vulnerability, women resilience — [Haifa](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/mens-vulnerability-womens-resilience-from-widowhood-to-late-life-/)
- [medium] British study raises that some widowers idealize their late wives, which may shape remarriage decisions (comparison with late spouse) — [Bennett, Arnott, Soulsby 2013](https://www.liverpool.ac.uk/~kmb/MyPublishedPapers/BennettArnottSoulsby2013.pdf)
- [medium, small and culturally specific] 14 Arab Muslim widowers in Israel remarried in their 70s, with caregiving a central motive — [PMC10736451](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10736451/). Do not generalize.
- [medium] Israeli qualitative study: adult children of repartnering parents report loyalty conflict and comparison between deceased parent and new partner — [Haifa](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/when-late-life-repartnering-and-parental-death-intertwine-adult-c/)
- [anecdotal] Practitioner commentary describes adult children fearing a new partner will take their inheritance; advice is honest conversation (popular press, no controlled study) — [CS Monitor 2005](https://csmonitor.com/2005/0608/p11s01-lifp.html)
- [anecdotal] Men's-advice site lists post-divorce fears including that dating again opens the door to deeper hurt — [The Modest Man](https://www.themodestman.com/hidden-dating-fears-men-over-40/)
- [anecdotal] Therapist column: midlife divorced clients need a coherent story of the past marriage; otherwise they may choose a partner mainly as "not my ex" and stay reactive — [Psychotherapy Networker](https://psychotherapynetworker.org/article/dating-midlife)

### Inferences
- Likely male fears at this stage: being compared with the ex or late spouse, financial exposure, adult-children conflict, caregiving; likely female concerns: losing hard-won independence. These are inferences from the above and not directly measured in 40-something daters.

### Gaps
- No survey data on men 40+ fear of stepfatherhood, inheritance, or caregiving specifically; no comparable women 40+ survey found.

## 3. What is said on the second date and after (disclosure, ex talk, kids, values, exclusivity)

### Takeaway
Sourced material is mostly generic advice, not 40+ research. Consensus advice: keep ex talk light, mention kids positively, avoid heavy topics early, and clarify goals before drifting.

### Cited Findings
- [anecdotal] Generic second-date advice: long complaints about an ex can push a date away; keep family/kids talk positive and light — [eHarmony](https://www.eharmony.ca/dating-advice/getting-to-know/second-date/); [Regain](https://regain.us/advice/dating/8-ideas-for-what-to-talk-about-on-a-second-date-to-get-to-know-them-better)
- [anecdotal] A dating survey reportedly found marriage, career and children the topics seen as most inappropriate on second dates (secondary report; original not verified) — via search summary of [Essence](https://www.essence.com/love/relationships/how-guarantee-second-date)
- [anecdotal] Dating-at-50 guide recommends clarifying your goal (casual companionship, long-term partner, remarriage) before dating to avoid misunderstandings — [Lash Lounge guide](https://old.thelashlounge.com/?p=19034) (low-quality site)
- [anecdotal] Introducing kids: sources converge on roughly 6-12 months of committed dating (one practitioner says 9-12), gradually; this is convention not research, and not specific to 40+ — [Illinois Lawyer Now](https://www.illinoislawyernow.com/2020/12/when-should-i-introduce-a-new-partner-to-my-children-after-divorce/)
- [medium] Pew (2019 survey, pub. 2020): 29% of online daters 50+ said someone kept contacting them after they said no (36% ages 35-49, 43% ages 18-34); 22% of 50+ received unsolicited explicit content vs 42% of 18-34 — via [Statista chart citing Pew](https://statista.com/statistics/809587/us-users-negative-online-dating-app-or-service-experience-by-type)

### Inferences
- Second-date content for 40+ plausibly includes a light version of "the ex story", kids, work and what each wants, but I found no verbatim examples or forum threads I could verify; none are invented here.

### Gaps
- No therapist column or forum thread specific to the "defining the relationship" talk, quoted examples, or politics/values on second dates. No data on how men 40+ signal interest or disinterest.

## 4. Ghosting, breadcrumbing and slow fade

### Takeaway
I could not find Pew or other reliable ghosting prevalence for 40+ specifically. Available figures are from younger samples or commercial surveys.

### Cited Findings
- [medium] Spanish peer-reviewed study of young adults: about 2 in 10 had experienced and initiated ghosting in the last 12 months; online dating use, more short-term relationships and online surveillance raised the likelihood of experiencing and initiating ghosting/breadcrumbing — [Navarro et al.](https://revistas.uma.es/index.php/espsi/article/download/9960/13115?inline=1)
- [medium] UK survey (Currys, via Statista): 17.45% of male and 19.2% of female online dating users had ghosted someone — [Statista](https://statista.com/statistics/1466546/uk-online-dating-users-ghosting-gender)
- [anecdotal/low] Plenty of Fish: almost 80% of 18-33 millennials report being ghosted (commercial survey) — [Global Dating Insights](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/news/over-80-of-daters-say-they-have-been-ghosted-when-searching-for-love/)
- Rejected: a 63% figure on a Columbia-hosted blog could not be verified; do not use.

### Inferences
- Reasons for ghosting among 40+ (avoidance of conflict, lingering attachment to ex, time pressure) are not evidenced here.

### Gaps
- No age-broken ghosting data (Pew 2023 report was not retrieved). Nothing on why men 40+ pull back or lean in, beyond the repartnering motives in section 2.

## 5. Intimacy and sex after 40/50

### Takeaway
Best available data is an older AARP survey (2009); newer age-specific data not found. ED evidence is general and not tied to new-partner situations.

### Cited Findings
- [medium, 2009 data] AARP Sex, Romance and Relationships (n=1,487 aged 45+, phone): sexual satisfaction 43% vs 51% in 2004; weekly intercourse 28%, monthly 40%; 84% of weekly-sex respondents satisfied vs 59% of those with sex once or twice a month; male satisfaction fell from 60% (45-49) to 26% (70+), women from 48% to 27%; financial worry cited as a negative change — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/research/topics/life/info-2014/srr_09.html); [NBC summary](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/sexual-satisfaction-ebbs-those-45-older-flna1C9448356)
- [medium, unverified original] Massachusetts Male Aging Study cited secondhand: ED incidence about 17% in men in their 40s to over 75% at 70-79 — via [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/health%2Ferectile-dysfunction-and-self-medication-the-dangerous-growing-pattern-among-older-men-4032161); verify before use
- [medium] Systematic review: median ED prevalence 20.0% among men with anxiety disorders, with high heterogeneity and unclear causal direction — [PMC8964411](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8964411/)
- [medium] Match/Kinsey Singles in America 2025 (not age-specific): 72% say they can assess chemistry within the first three dates — [Global Dating Insights](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/featured/matchs-singles-in-america-study-reveals-modern-dating-culture-shifts/)

### Inferences
- Sexual-health worries and body image are plausibly part of the early-relationship "nervous" period, but no source here measures body-image worry or consent-communication in 40+ daters.

### Gaps
- No evidence retrieved on body image, consent communication, or timing of first sex for 40+. No medical advice given.
