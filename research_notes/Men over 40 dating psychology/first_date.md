# First dates when one or both people are 40+: how men 40+ talk and behave

Method note: WebSearch only (secondary summaries). One WebFetch of the Boothby liking-gap PDF failed (DNS failure, ENOTFOUND), so no primary paper full text was read; effect sizes and sample details below come from search summaries. Confidence tags: [strong] = replicated or peer-reviewed; [medium] = single study, survey of unclear quality, or consistent expert advice; [anecdotal] = blogs, coaching or listicles. No Reddit/forum threads were retrievable, so there are no real scripts or quotes. None are invented. Almost all research below is on younger (student) samples, not 40+.

## 1. What research says about first-date and speed-date conversation: which behaviours predict "click" and a second date

### Takeaway
The best-supported predictors are asking follow-up questions, perceived responsiveness, and reciprocal turn-taking disclosure. Pre-date traits predict who is popular, but not which specific pair clicks. People also underestimate how much their date likes them. All evidence is mostly from students, not 40+ (a gap).

### Cited Findings
- Huang, Yeomans, Brooks, Minson & Gino, "It doesn't hurt to ask": across three studies of live conversations, people who asked more questions were better liked; perceived responsiveness explained the effect. In speed dating, those who asked more questions were more likely to get partners to agree to a second date. [strong/medium: peer-reviewed, student samples] — [Search summary of the paper](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/person/03f350eb-aa15-4e87-88ab-793bbafa97a8)
- Speed-dating sample reported as 110 men and women, each on 15-19 dates, with a yes/no second-date decision recorded. Follow-up questions specifically were a strong predictor for both sexes. — [Huang et al. poster (UBC)](https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/presentations/2016-Poster-Huang-Karen-conversation-responsiveness-machinelearning~.pdf). Popular coverage cites ~2,000 conversations and a "15 questions" threshold; I could not verify these, so do not use them. [medium]
- People do not anticipate that asking questions raises liking (possibly worry about seeming rude). [medium] — same Harvard DASH summary above.
- Boothby, Cooney, Sandstrom & Clark (2018), "The Liking Gap in Conversations" (Psychological Science): after conversations, people underestimate how much partners liked them and enjoyed their company; held for conversations from 2 to 45 minutes and persisted among dorm-mates for months; driven by harsher self-critical thoughts; shyness widens it; independent coders could judge liking accurately. [strong] — [Paper PDF (not fetched)](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/The-Liking-Gap-in-Conversations.pdf); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/illusion-chasers/people-like-you-more-than-you-know)
- Joel, Eastwick & Finkel (2017): machine learning on 100+ pre-date self-report measures from two speed-dating studies (4-minute dates) predicted 4-18% of actor variance and 7-27% of partner variance (general popularity/pickiness) but could not predict pair-specific ("relationship") attraction. Eastwick compares romantic desire to an earthquake rather than a chemical reaction. [strong] — [Discover Magazine](https://discovermagazine.com/health/can-a-computer-predict-romantic-chemistry); [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797617714580)
- Eastwick & Finkel (2008): stated ideals (attractiveness, earning prospects) show sex differences, but did not predict actual in-person romantic interest, and no sex differences in what actually drove interest. [strong] — [Gwern PDF](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/sociology/2008-eastwick.pdf) (via search summary)
- Eastwick, Finkel, Mochon & Ariely: expressed desire can backfire when it is unselective (generalized) rather than unique to the person; speed-dating sample of 1,561 students. [medium] — [Ariely lab PDF](https://web.mit.edu/ariely/www/MIT/Papers/reciprocity.pdf) (search snippet only)
- Tidwell, Eastwick & Finkel (2013): perceived, not actual, similarity predicts initial attraction in speed dating. [medium] — [Kellogg](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2013/perceived-not-actual-similarity-predicts-initial-attraction-in-a/)
- Aron et al. (1997) "36 questions": pairs of strangers answering escalating, reciprocal personal questions felt closer than pairs on small-talk questions, regardless of attitude similarity or expectations; authors caution this does not produce commitment or loyalty; college-student sample. [strong for closeness; no evidence on dating outcomes] — [Anglia Ruskin summary](https://aru.ac.uk/news/can-36-questions-really-change-your-love-life); [Psych Central](https://psychcentral.com/blog/want-to-be-close-to-someone-ask-these-36-questions/)
- Sprecher et al. (2013) "Taking turns: Reciprocal self-disclosure promotes liking in initial interactions" (title confirmed; design not seen). [medium] — [Sprecher CV](https://about.illinoisstate.edu/sprecher/files/2022/06/Susan-Sprecher-Vita-June-2022.pdf)
- A small undergraduate project (N=41 pairs) found men's disclosure predicted women's attraction after controlling for his appearance. [anecdotal: student project, not peer reviewed] — [SFU UGRS](https://journals.lib.sfu.ca/index.php/ugrs/article/view/2293)

### Inferences
- Practical reading (non-manipulative): genuine curiosity with follow-up questions plus matched, mutual disclosure is the best-evidenced conversational pattern; one-sided talking or one-sided interrogation is not supported.
- The liking gap suggests anxious 40+ men (and women) likely under-read how well the date is going; a reasonable inference, not tested in 40+ samples.
- "Click" is only partly predictable in advance, so profile-matching is weak and the live interaction matters.

### Gaps
- No study found of speed-dating or first-date conversation specifically in 40-65 samples.
- Primary papers not read (fetch failed); Sprecher's design unverified; Pew has no first-date conversation data that I found.

## 2. People 40+: topics, "too much too soon", divorced and widowed disclosure

### Takeaway
Advice sources agree on a rough rule: name the facts (divorced, kids, widowed) briefly and honestly, defer the full story and the grievances. Evidence is almost entirely coaching content, not research.

### Cited Findings
- Never speaking negatively of an ex or dwelling on the ex is the most consistent piece of advice; one coach says people who re-partner quickly tend to dwell on the former partner, so vent to a friend beforehand. [anecdotal/medium: consistent but unsourced] — [HuffPost UK](https://huffingtonpost.co.uk/entry/dating-fails_b_6461888); [The Modest Man](https://www.themodestman.com/?p=658725)
- Divorce timing: Marie Claire suggests holding off on divorce detail unless kids are central; DatingAdvice.com says full divorce discussion is too soon on a first date, until considering exclusivity; one Sixty and Me post says it matters more after 4-5 dates. [anecdotal; commercial] — [Marie Claire](https://www.marieclaire.com/sex-love/a2868/what-not-to-say-on-a-first-date/); [DatingAdvice](https://www.datingadvice.com/advice/3-first-date-topics-for-baby-boomer-men-to-avoid); [Sixty and Me](https://sixtyandme.com/first-date-blunders/comment-page-2)
- Too early: medical, financial or family details, and drinking too much; lying about age; interview-style rapid-fire questions; talking about yourself too much. [anecdotal] — [Sixty and Me](https://sixtyandme.com/?p=28733); [FODMAP Everyday slideshow (low quality)](https://www.fodmapeveryday.com/15-mistakes-older-men-cant-afford-to-make-when-dating/); [The Modest Man](https://www.themodestman.com/?p=658725)
- Widowed: Chapter 2 (a dating service for widowed people) survey of ~15,000 widowed members: 90% said memories of the late partner influence new relationships, 45% often feel guilt or hesitation about dating, 54% comfortable discussing widowhood on a first date. [medium-low: self-selected, commercial] — [Chapter 2](https://www.chapter2dating.app/talking-about-your-late-partner-when-dating/)
- Widowed guidance: share gradually as trust builds (Chapter 2); JDate suggests telling on the first date but not the whole story; an AARP-quoted sex therapist advises the date not ask for details of the death or funeral, and respond to mentions of the late spouse warmly (e.g. "He sounds like he mattered a lot"). [anecdotal/medium] — [JDate](https://jlife.jdate.com/dating-after-a-death/); [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/family-relationships/in-the-mood-advice-for-dating-widows/)
- OurTime (site for 50+) survey of over-50s: poor health 78%, questionable finances 76%, unattractiveness 75% most alarming in a date. [medium-low: commercial] — as reported in [DatingAdvice](https://www.datingadvice.com/studies/dating-after-55-older-adults-research-matches-reject-dishonesty-and-wont-tolerate-rudeness) (search summary; exact OurTime link not retrieved)

### Inferences
- Health, money and exes are the high-stakes topics in this age group because they signal risk to the partner; this fits why advice says to keep them factual and brief early.
- The advice set looks recycled across sites; consistency does not equal evidence.

### Gaps
- No research on what topics men 40+ actually raise (kids, careers, parents, retirement); no therapist-authored primary material found; no empirical data on whether early divorce/widowhood disclosure lowers second-date odds.

## 3. Surveys: what women (and men) dislike on first dates; source quality

### Takeaway
The only large, usable dislike data are commercial Match "Singles in America" figures, not age-split. Treat as indicative only.

### Cited Findings
- Match Singles in America (women's top turn-offs): checking phone too often 73%, trying too hard to impress 41%, talking too much about finances 34%; phone-checking was top for both sexes. Not split by age. [medium: large commercial survey] — [WCCB summary of Match data](https://www.wccbcharlotte.com/?p=302918)
- A Substack claims safety is the first priority of women over 40 on first dates; no study cited. [anecdotal] — [Retired Hot Girl Substack](https://retiredhotgirl.substack.com/p/the-1st-date-list-and-toxic-internet)
- Claim that chemistry was highest when the woman was the main subject and the man showed he listened: unsourced popular article. [anecdotal] — via search summary; do not use without primary source.
- AARP 2012 online dating survey (1,000 adults): it is about online dating in general, not first-date behaviour. [medium] — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/research/topics/life/info-2014/online-dating-sites-survey/)

### Inferences
- Phone use, boasting/money talk and self-absorption recur as dislikes; these overlap with research on responsiveness.

### Gaps
- No Pew, Hinge, Bumble, eHarmony, YouGov or AARP data on first-date dislikes by age found; nothing on women 40+ disliking ex-talk, politics, or work talk specifically.

## 4. Scripts and examples (openers, silence, self-description, asking to meet again)

### Takeaway
Only generic coaching advice found; no real transcripts, Reddit threads or 40+-specific scripts could be retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Use open-ended questions and follow up on answers; avoid rapid-fire interview mode; easy starters are travel, weekend plans, food, pets. [anecdotal] — [Hello! Magazine](https://www.hellomagazine.com/brides/869153/first-date-conversation-tips); [Muddy Matches](https://articles.muddymatches.co.uk/2016/06/12/what-to-talk-about-on-a-date)
- A few comfortable silences are normal; an activity date (bowling, mini-golf) reduces silence; phones away. [anecdotal] — same sources.
- A Dynamite News/DNYUZ headline, "6 things to do on a first date if you want a second one" (Sept 2026), exists but I did not read it. [unverified] — [DNYUZ](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/30/6-things-to-do-on-a-first-date-if-you-want-a-second-one/)

### Gaps
- No verbatim "tell me about yourself" answers, no data on how men 40+ ask for a second date.

## 5. Who pays, date length, endings, safety and etiquette

### Takeaway
Pay norms are split; the most common US answer is still that the man pays, though women lean to "whoever asked". Match data suggests ~2.5 hours is the best length for a second date (older data). Safety advice is consistent.

### Cited Findings
- YouGov (US): 35% say the man should always pay (men 40%, women 29%); women's most common answer was whoever initiated pays (37%). [medium: reputable pollster, not age-split] — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/19659-40-men-think-men-should-always-pay-bill-first-date)
- Ipsos: 43% of men who pay do so out of habit. [medium] — [Ipsos](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/men-and-women-dont-agree-dates-sex-heartbreak)
- YouGov UK reports women do not generally find men paying sexist or patronising and most men are happy to pay. [medium] — [YouGov UK](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/16412-Women-still-happy-to-be-bought-dinner-on-first-dat)
- Australia (RateCity, 2016): 71% of men expect to pay vs 4% of women [low-medium: commercial, dated] — [RateCity](https://www.ratecity.com.au/media-room/first-date-etiquette-pay)
- Match Singles in America (6th edition, ~2016, ~5,000 US singles): odds of a second date rise with first-date length up to about 2.5 hours, then fall. [medium: commercial, dated, single source] — [KIRO7](https://www.kiro7.com/news/trending-now/10-things-we-learned-from-matchcoms-singles-survey/63844688)
- Safety (AARP and others): video chat first; meet in a busy public place; tell a friend and check in; own transport; hold back last name and personal details until after the first date; set limits such as drink count; leave if something feels off. [medium: consistent, reputable] — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/family-relationships/dating-dos-and-donts); [Univ. of Minnesota PDF](https://aurora.umn.edu/sites/aurora.umn.edu/files/2024-03/TipsForSafeOnlineDating.pdf)
- Romance-scam risk is cited by AARP-based reporting (11 million Americans over 50 drawn into a romance scam); source is secondary. [medium-low] — [DatingAdvice](https://www.datingadvice.com/studies/dating-after-55-older-adults-research-matches-reject-dishonesty-and-wont-tolerate-rudeness)

### Inferences
- Offering to split, or the initiator paying, matches the more neutral-looking poll answers; the date's reaction matters more than the rule.

### Gaps
- No pay-norm data for 40+, no data on how first dates end or typical length in 40+, no Pew first-date data found; Match length figure is old.
