# South Asian men 40+ (Pakistan, India, UK/US/Gulf diaspora): marriage, second marriage, divorce, emotional life, online spaces

Method note: about 12 WebSearch/vidIQ calls, 2026-10. WebFetch was NOT attempted, so every finding below comes from search-result summaries, not from full-page reads. Verify figures at the primary source before publishing. Evidence tags: [strong] = survey, court record or peer-reviewed; [medium] = news feature with named data, or small qualitative study; [anecdotal] = single voice, forum post, opinion piece. Almost nothing found is specific to men aged 40+, which is itself a gap.

## 1. What do surveys, ethnographies and news features say about South Asian men's views on marriage, divorce, remarriage and emotional life?

### Takeaway
Hard data on men 40+ and divorce/remarriage attitudes is thin. The best quantitative anchors are NFHS (India, men's attitudes to wife-beating, a proxy for patriarchal norms), Pew India 2021 (endogamy norms) and the BACP 2026 survey (therapy stigma). Most of the rest is news features and small qualitative studies.

### Cited Findings
- [strong] NFHS-5 (2019-21): about 44% of Indian men justified wife-beating for at least one of seven listed reasons (going out without telling him, neglecting house/children, arguing, refusing sex, bad cooking, suspected infidelity, disrespecting in-laws). This was 42% in NFHS-4 and 50% in 2005-06. Women's figure fell from 52% to 45.4%. Karnataka 81.9% of men and Telangana 70.4%. Sample is men 15-54 (BMC) or 15-49 (news), not 40+ only. — [BMC Public Health 2024 via PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10829205/); [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/large-number-indians-believe-its-ok-husband-beat-wife-163801)
- [strong] Pew India 2021 (29,999 adults, Nov 2019-Mar 2020): 99% of married respondents say their spouse shares their religion; about 65% say it is very important to stop interfaith marriage by men of their community (Hindus 65%, Muslims 76%, Sikhs 58%, Jains 59%, Christians 35%). Figures are as relayed by The Quint; Pew has no divorce or remarriage data in this report. — [The Quint](https://thequint.com/amp/story/news/india/pew-survey-67-percent-indians-stop-interfaith-marriage-hindu-muslim-sikh-jain); primary: [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2021/06/29/religion-in-india/)
- [medium] BACP Public Perceptions Survey (reported 2026): 51% of South Asian men think therapy is still stigmatised versus 44% of men overall; about 1 in 10 who considered therapy but did not go cited fear of what others think. Practitioner survey, not peer-reviewed. — [BACP](https://www.bacp.co.uk/news/news-from-bacp/2026/10-july-stigma-still-stops-many-south-asian-men-from-seeking-therapy/)
- [medium] Qualitative study of British-Bangladeshi Muslim men aged 22-59 in London with anxiety/depression: stigma, traditional cultural expectations and low trust in services were main barriers. Bangladeshi men were under a third of NHS Talking Therapies referrals in 2019-20 (as cited). — [London Met repository](https://repository.londonmet.ac.uk/7486); [Mental Elf](https://www.nationalelfservice.net/populations-and-settings/black-and-minority-ethnic/british-bangladeshi-men-mental-health/)
- [medium] A UK PhD on Pakistani men's help-seeking notes most South Asian mental-health research focuses on women or the whole population, with a "dearth" of work on men. A scoping review cited by Mental Elf found few interventions aimed specifically at South Asian men. — [London Met](https://repository.londonmet.ac.uk/7486); [Mental Elf](https://www.nationalelfservice.net/populations-and-settings/black-and-minority-ethnic/british-bangladeshi-men-mental-health/)
- [medium] India men's rights movement: began in the 1990s (Kolkata, Mumbai, Lucknow) in reaction to alleged misuse of IPC 498A (cruelty by husband/in-laws). NCRB 2020: 1,11,549 cases registered under 498A; police classed 5,520 as false and 16,151 closed for false fact/law or insufficient evidence (this is not a direct measure of fabrication). Activists claim much higher "false" rates, which a News Minute column treats sceptically. Most sources are advocacy-adjacent or opinion. — [Wikipedia overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men%27s_rights_movement_in_India); [Feminism in India](https://feminisminindia.com/2023/06/01/the-rise-of-mens-rights-activism-in-india-a-feminist-issue-in-misogynist-garb/); [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/opinion/welcome-mra-land-lonely-ex-wives-sisters-who-elope-milkmen-and-scooter-tires-36196); [Caravan](https://caravanmagazine.in/gender-and-sexuality/first-conference-on-national-commission-for-men)
- [medium] Pakistan family-court load is rising: Rawalpindi courts recorded 10,103 family cases Jan-Sep 2026 incl. 1,246 divorce decrees to women; Sindh khula filings rose from 11,639 (2020) to 16,287 in a later year per The Quint. No single national divorce database exists; researchers caution rises may reflect awareness and access, not just breakdown. — [ProPakistani 2026](https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/01/rawalpindi-sees-record-rise-in-divorce-and-family-cases/); [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2632363/divorce-khula-cases-swamp-family-courts); [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/amp/story/opinion/marriage-laws-in-pakistan-why-are-more-women-in-karachi-seeking-divorce)
- [medium] Supreme Court of Pakistan: family courts cannot convert a dissolution suit into khula without the wife's clear informed consent. — [stateofchildren.com summary](https://stateofchildren.com/?p=5674)

### Inferences
- A creator speaking to men 40+ is likely addressing men raised in a context where roughly 4 in 10 Indian men (NFHS) endorse husband authority via violence in at least one scenario, so lecturing tone will be rejected; reflective, non-judgmental tone is safer. (Inference from NFHS plus therapy-stigma data.)
- The rising female-initiated divorce and khula filings in Pakistan probably feed male anxieties about losing wife, children and money, and feed "men are victims" content; the 498A debate in India is the analogue. (Inference; no direct survey of 40+ men found.)

### Gaps
- No Gallup Pakistan or Pew data on men's attitudes to divorce or remarriage surfaced. Not searched with Gallup-specific queries.
- No survey disaggregated for men 40+ on marriage satisfaction or remarriage intent.
- No peer-reviewed ethnography of Indian men's rights groups found (only journalism). No data on suicide linked to marital disputes retrieved.
- Gulf-diaspora men: nothing found. US South Asian men: nothing found. Not searched in depth.

## 2. What does male-to-male talk look like (topics, tone, taboos)?

### Takeaway
I found no direct ethnography of dera/chai/masjid/barbershop/WhatsApp-group talk among South Asian men 40+. What exists is indirect: help-seeking studies (stigma, restraint), men's rights forums (grievance framing), and content-market signals. Treat everything here as low-confidence.

### Cited Findings
- [medium] Help-seeking research shows "what will people think" and traditional masculine expectations suppress disclosure to services (see Q1 BACP and London studies). — [BACP](https://www.bacp.co.uk/news/news-from-bacp/2026/10-july-stigma-still-stops-many-south-asian-men-from-seeking-therapy/); [London Met](https://repository.londonmet.ac.uk/7486)
- [anecdotal] Indian MRA online spaces frame ex-wives and in-laws as adversaries and law (498A, dowry law) as weaponised; critics describe the tone as misogynist. — [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/opinion/welcome-mra-land-lonely-ex-wives-sisters-who-elope-milkmen-and-scooter-tires-36196); [Feminism in India](https://feminisminindia.com/2023/06/01/the-rise-of-mens-rights-activism-in-india-a-feminist-issue-in-misogynist-garb/)
- [anecdotal] A Karachi matchmaker (older Tribune feature) says male clients, including 60-year-old divorced men, ask for young unmarried brides, and some want a younger/better-looking second wife partly to show off to the ex. — [Express Tribune, "Shaadi 2.0"](https://tribune.com.pk/story/379627/shaadi-2-0) (pre-2015, outside the date window; use as colour only)

### Inferences
- Likely pattern (unverified): men 40+ discuss money, children, health and in-laws in jokes or indirect complaints and avoid direct emotional disclosure; grievance spaces (MRA, WhatsApp forwards) are where sharper talk happens. This is hypothesis, not finding.

### Gaps
- No source on slang, humour, Roman Urdu/Hinglish idioms, or taboos in male peer talk. I did not invent examples. Needs native-speaker input or comment-mining of a few Urdu/Hindi channels.
- No data on barbershop/dera/masjid conversations.

## 3. "Dusri shaadi", divorced men in the rishta market, widower remarriage, age gaps

### Takeaway
Consistent but anecdotal signal: divorced and widowed men remarry more easily than women and can ask for younger brides, yet are not immune to stigma. Widowers remarry more than widows (older census data; small Pakistani study).

### Cited Findings
- [anecdotal] Divorced men generally find remarriage easier than women, per a matchmaker quoted in a Karachi feature. — [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/379627/shaadi-2-0)
- [anecdotal] A Pakistani man on r/pakistan says a short marriage ending over concealed health problems is an instant dealbreaker with prospective families. Single anecdote. — [r/pakistan daily rishta thread](https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/pakistan/comments/1jme1fw/daily_rishta_thread)
- [medium] Commentary says remarriage was widely frowned upon but attitudes are gradually shifting; scrutiny falls hardest on divorced women. — [Pakistan Observer](https://pakobserver.net/pakistani-societys-acceptance-of-remarriage/); [Friday Times 2023](https://thefridaytimes.com/07-Oct-2023/women-s-remarriage-and-freedom-in-pakistan-the-triumph-of-choice)
- [medium] India 1971 census-based estimate: about one third of ever-widowed women and nearly two thirds of ever-widowed men were currently remarried. Old data. — [IHSN catalogue](https://catalog.ihsn.org/citations/27296)
- [medium] Small Pakhtun qualitative study (10 widows, 10 widowers): remarriage of elderly widows is rare; very few widowers remarry, depending on health, authority, finances and family background. — [JGSI](https://jgsi.fjwu.edu.pk/jgsi/article/view/232)
- [medium] Muslim marriage practices in Britain: nikah-only marriages are common; one legal commentator says some are motivated by a husband keeping the door open to a second marriage or avoiding obligations, but "no one dominant factor". Akhter v Khan (2020): a nikah-only ceremony is a "non-marriage" under English law, leaving women without financial remedies. — [Islamic Law blog](https://islamiclaw.blog/2019/12/06/muslim-marriage-and-divorce-practices-in-contemporary-britain-part-7-conclusions-and-further-observations/); [Nordic J. Law & Religion](https://tidsskrift.dk/nnjlsr/article/view/125690)
- [anecdotal] Celebrity cases (Jibran Nasir marrying a divorcee; Behroze Sabzwari controversy) show public double standards on remarriage. — [ARY News](https://backend.arynews.tv/jibran-nasir-on-marrying-mansha-pasha-after-divorce/)

### Inferences
- Age-gap expectations by 40+ men (younger brides) are probably a real friction point but only anecdote supports it; do not state prevalence.
- Content that honestly addresses "what a second wife may reasonably expect" would fill an empty niche (see Q4).

### Gaps
- No numbers on how many men 40+ use rishta aunties, Shaadi.com, Jodi or Muzz after divorce, nor success rates.
- No study isolated Pakistani-heritage UK men taking second wives.
- No Jodi or Shaadi.com user research found; Muzz data is company self-report only (see Q5).

## 5. Cultural and legal cautions for a creator

### Takeaway
Pakistan law requires written Arbitration Council permission for a second marriage; violation carries civil (immediate dower) and criminal consequences. Content should not encourage evading it. Religious, defamation and sexual-content cautions below are mostly general-knowledge inferences, not sourced.

### Cited Findings
- [strong] Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961 s.6(1): no man may contract another marriage during an existing one without prior written permission of the Arbitration Council; the marriage cannot be registered without it. s.6(5)(a): breach makes the husband liable to pay the entire dower to the first wife immediately. s.6(5)(b): criminal penalty. Reported outcomes vary: Lahore family court, March 2024, 7 months simple imprisonment plus Rs500,000 fine, rejecting infertility of first wife as defence; Peshawar, 3 months plus Rs5,000; a Tribune column cites 6 months plus Rs200,000. The statutory maximum was not confirmed in these results. — [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/1821920); [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/1828682); [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/article/93887/to-thrash-or-not-to-thrash-marrying-again-without-the-first-wifes-permission); [Pakistan case law (Mujahida v Alamgir 2025 PHC)](https://pakcaselaw.duckdns.org/case/195852/mst-mujahida-vs-alamgir-2025-phc-184)
- [medium] A 2020 fact-check found false the claim that the Council of Islamic Ideology removed first-wife permission; headlines were taken out of context. Misinformation risk on this topic is high. — [Soch Fact Check](https://www.sochfactcheck.com/?p=1656)
- [strong] UK: nikah-only is a non-marriage in English law (Akhter v Khan 2020). — [Nordic J. Law & Religion](https://tidsskrift.dk/nnjlsr/article/view/125690)
- [medium] Criticism of men's rights framing: critics describe MRA content as misogynist, and 498A "false case" statistics are contested. Repeating such claims uncritically invites backlash. — [Feminism in India](https://feminisminindia.com/2023/06/01/the-rise-of-mens-rights-activism-in-india-a-feminist-issue-in-misogynist-garb/)

### Inferences (not sourced; check with a lawyer or local advisers)
- Do not advise or imply that a man can take a second wife secretly; legal and social risk is real in Pakistan, and in the UK nikah-only leaves the wife unprotected.
- Avoid explicit sexual content and keep religious rulings attributed to scholars rather than self-issued; Pakistani and Gulf platform and legal norms around "obscenity" and blasphemy are strict. Defamation: do not name exes, in-laws or real people in anecdotes; anonymise composites.
- Do not invent quotes from clerics; use clip attribution only when verified.

### Gaps
- Not verified: Pakistan cyber-crime law (PECA) specifics, Indian law on second marriage (Hindu Marriage Act bigamy s.494 IPC) and Gulf rules on polygyny and content. Gulf legal position unsearched.
- Custody law (Guardians and Wards Act, Family Courts Act) and gender-disaggregated custody data not found; Rawalpindi data covers only women's custody claims.

## Overall not reached / limitations
- No full-page fetches; all from search summaries. Gallup Pakistan, Pew diaspora, UK Muslim dating research (e.g. academic studies on Muzz users), US South Asian men's studies, Gulf diaspora, Scroll/Hindu/BBC Urdu-Hindi features: not retrieved. Humour and slang vocabulary, tone of male peer talk, and fears (shame, money, children, health) are not documented by any source found, so only help-seeking stigma (BACP, London studies) and anecdotal rishta evidence support the "fear of social judgment" claim.
