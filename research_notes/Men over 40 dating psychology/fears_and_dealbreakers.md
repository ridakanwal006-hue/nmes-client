# Fears, Insecurities and Dealbreakers of Men 40+ in Dating (and Women's Dealbreakers About Men 40+)

Method note: WebFetch was tried once (themodestman.com) and failed with a DNS error (ENOTFOUND); all other findings come from WebSearch result summaries only, not full-text reads. Evidence ratings: STRONG = peer-reviewed/large probability-type survey; MEDIUM = reputable org survey or company data, unverified methods, or secondary report; ANECDOTAL = blog/opinion/commercially commissioned. Searches did NOT locate a Cleveland Clinic survey, a Match Singles in America over-40 dealbreaker table, or an AARP dating-specific study. Treat those as gaps.

## 1. What do surveys and studies show about dating anxiety, rejection sensitivity and body image in midlife men?

### Takeaway
Direct evidence on men 40+ specifically is thin. The best-supported items are: UK survey that 6 in 10 men feel insecure dating (rejection, age, appearance top drivers); a 2026 Evolution and Human Behavior paper on single men linking body-image beliefs to dating comfort; and AARP data showing midlife loneliness is highest at 45-49 and now higher in men. Most "midlife-man fear lists" online are anecdotal.

### Cited Findings
- Relate/eharmony 2021 report: six in ten men feel insecure when dating, with fear of rejection, their age and appearance the top contributing factors (sample age profile not confirmed in my search; all-adult UK men) [MEDIUM] — [Relate](https://www.relate.org.uk/get-help/60-men-have-felt-insecure-when-dating-rejection-their-biggest-fear)
- Frederick, Reynolds, Smart, Garcia & Puts, Evolution and Human Behavior (2026, "in press" per listing): two online-panel studies of single men (N=1919; N=435, one census-matched US sample). After controls, men's beliefs about muscularity and penis size were most consistently related to dating-comfort outcomes, followed by body fat and facial masculinity. Only abstract/listing seen, not full text; age breakdown for 40+ unknown [STRONG for peer review, but 40+ applicability unverified] — [Chapman Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.chapman.edu/psychology_articles/489)
- 2021 UK study of 501 emerging adults (Body Image): negative body image and social physique anxiety each correlated with dating anxiety, but body image added little once social physique anxiety was accounted for. Young sample, not 40+ [STRONG, wrong age group] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34749263/)
- 738 men aged 18-76 (Victoria University thesis): poor male body image predicted by BMI, drive for muscularity and penis-size dissatisfaction; gap between how men judge their bodies among other men and as sexual partners [MEDIUM, thesis] — [VU repository](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/21310/)
- 2008 Journal of Health Psychology, 200 men online: dissatisfaction across six body aspects, main worries weight, penis size, height [MEDIUM, older, small] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18987089/)
- Hair loss: five-country European telephone survey of men 18-45: 47% reported hair loss; 62% of those agreed it could affect self-esteem; 43% linked it to loss of attractiveness; 38% of those not in stable relationships reported reduced confidence [MEDIUM] — [Univ. of Miami record](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-psychosocial-impact-of-hair-loss/991031598692702976)
- Hair clinic polls (commercial): 73% of 800 balding men said less luck dating than hairy friends; 40% said confidence approaching women hurt [ANECDOTAL, commissioned]; contradicted by Superdrug survey where ~68% of men and ~71% of women said hair loss had no impact on love life [ANECDOTAL, also commercial] — [The Private Clinic](https://www.theprivateclinic.co.uk/?p=48816); [Superdrug](https://onlinedoctor.superdrug.com/losing-your-hair-but-not-your-mojo/)
- AARP Aug 2025 survey (n=3,276 US adults 45+): loneliness 40%, up from 35%; peaks at 45-49 (49%), 50-59 (45%); men 42% vs women 37%; men 17% vs 13% no close friends; never-married/divorced/separated lonelier [STRONG, not dating-specific] — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/social-leisure/relationships/loneliness-social-connections-2025/)
- LBC opinion piece: 43% of men afraid to date again after divorce; survey unnamed [ANECDOTAL] — [LBC](https://www.lbc.co.uk/opinion/views/men-need-more-support-divorce-opinion/)
- Pew (2020 short read): ~57% of men who online dated in past 5 years felt they did not get enough messages vs 24% of women. A different summary cites 61% vs 44% for 2023 and I could not reconcile the two; verify before use [STRONG source, figures unreconciled] — [Pew 2020](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2020/02/06/10-facts-about-americans-and-online-dating/markdown); [Pew 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2023/02/02/the-experiences-of-u-s-online-daters/)
- Pew 2023 summary: men more likely to report positive digital-dating experiences than women (57% vs 48%); women, especially under 50, more likely to report unwanted behaviours [STRONG, secondhand summary] — [Pressreader summary](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/arkansas-democrat-gazette/20230214/282737706002766)

### Fear catalogue (evidence level per fear)
- Rejection: MEDIUM (Relate/eharmony; Pew message gap supports lived experience of low response).
- Age / "too old": MEDIUM (Relate lists age as top driver); see Q2 for age-preference data.
- Looks, baldness: MEDIUM-ANECDOTAL (academic hair-loss survey is 18-45 only; commercial polls conflict).
- Body/fitness, penis size, height: STRONG-MEDIUM (Frederick 2026; 2008 and thesis data) but samples not restricted to 40+.
- Sexual performance (erectile function etc.): not directly evidenced in my searches; gap.
- Money and status, baggage (ex, kids, debts), emotional vulnerability, commitment, being compared to a late spouse, losing independence, appearing desperate, tech incompetence, approach anxiety: ANECDOTAL only. They appear in self-help blogs such as The Modest Man (fear of another emotional injury, of being judged for dating too soon or too late after divorce, competing with younger men, feeling out of step with modern dating) but I could not open the page — [The Modest Man via search snippet](https://www.themodestman.com/hidden-dating-fears-men-over-40/). No peer-reviewed support found.
- Unverified claim: "only 30% of divorced men over 50 date within a year, per 2023 Pew" appears on a low-quality blog and could not be confirmed. Do not use — [source blog](https://blogs.uni-bremen.de/vigrx/?p=91)

### Inferences
- Because the 2026 paper and hair-loss survey are not 40+-specific, the safest framing is "men of all ages, with midlife likely similar or greater for hair/age worries."
- Pew message-gap data plausibly feeds rejection sensitivity and "go cold/avoid" behaviours, but I found no study that tests that link.
- Behavioural responses (avoiding dating, overcompensating, ghosting, joking, going cold, lying about age/height, old photos): only the lying/old-photos part has empirical support (Q3); the rest are untested in what I found.

### Gaps
- Cleveland Clinic survey: not found.
- Full text/age breakdown of Frederick et al. 2026 not read.
- No rejection-sensitivity study of men 40+ found; no sexual performance anxiety data in dating context; no evidence on late-spouse comparison, baggage fears.

## 2. Top dealbreakers women over 35/40 report about men; fair vs stereotype

### Takeaway
I could not find a primary survey breaking out women 40+ dealbreakers about men. General Match/Singles in America-type lists point to dishonesty, anger, untrustworthiness, substance abuse and abuse, which are behaviour-based. Age-preference data suggests women tend to message men around their own age, which counters the stereotype that 40+ men are shut out by age alone.

### Cited Findings
- Match 2024 data as reported by an aggregator (unverified, untraced to Match): dishonesty 70%, anger management issues 64%, untrustworthy 61%, substance abuse 58%, abusive behaviour 57%; women in their 30s-40s rank "wants kids alignment" among top three dealbreakers [ANECDOTAL/MEDIUM, secondary] — [ConnectedCouples](https://www.connectedcouples.app/blog/relationship-deal-breakers-statistics)
- Same aggregator: women report 8-10 dealbreakers on average vs 5-7 for men, attributed to Pew and Match, unverified [ANECDOTAL] — same source
- Older Match Singles in America sample (ages 21-76) was used in a psychology dealbreaker paper (17 options); no over-40 figures seen — [Jonason PDF](https://testweb.dpg.unipd.it/sites/dpg.unipd.it/files/jonason.pdf)
- OkCupid 2017 analysis: when women initiate, men reply more to older women (40-year-old woman: 60% reply from 25-year-old men vs 36% from 55-year-old men). Women tend to message and reply to men around their own age, with more responses to younger men after 35. Company data, critics (Yarkoni) note normalisation problems [MEDIUM] — [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/article/32552-okcupid-data-messaging-older-women-dating-apps); [Yarkoni](https://talyarkoni.org/blog/tag/singles/)
- 2018 study (Bruch et al.): women's desirability by age declines steeply from 18 to 65 (this concerns men's preference for women's youth, not women's dealbreakers) [MEDIUM, via news coverage] — [HotAir summary](https://hotair.com/headlines/2018/08/15/study-online-daters-women-peak-18-men-peak-50-oy-n319974)
- Korea (Duo, 1,000 single women 25-34): 57% would hesitate to date men in their 40s identifying with a youthful lifestyle. Non-English-speaking, younger women, not on-topic [MEDIUM, out of scope] — [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/survey-reveals-why-women-in-their-20s-and-30s-are-reluctant-to-date-young-40s-men/12454405)

### Inferences
- Fair concerns (behaviour/safety): dishonesty, anger, substance abuse, abuse, kids/child-wanting mismatch. Stereotype-prone: "baggage," baldness, income/status, age alone; no data found showing these top women 40+ lists.

### Gaps
- No Pew/AARP/Match table for women 40+ dealbreakers; no data on unemployment, ex-bitterness, emotional unavailability, phone/tech habits as dealbreakers; need Match's actual report.

## 3. Profile behaviours that cause failure

### Takeaway
Small embellishments (height, outdated photos) are very common; large age lies less so. Evidence on bitter-ex bios and negativity was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Cornell/Toma-Hancock research as summarised: most daters lie about at least one of age/height/weight; men add about half an inch to height; women shave ~8.5 lb; roughly 15-20% lie about age per one account; women's photos averaged about 1.25-1.5 years old; ~one-third of photos judged inaccurate; deceptive profiles use fewer first-person "I" pronouns [MEDIUM, older studies, secondary reporting] — [UW Alumni](https://uwalumni.com/news/online-dating-research); [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/finding-love-the-scientific-take/201712/lying-online)

### Inferences
- Photo/age/height lying is a way men manage the fears of age/looks, but it risks the "dishonesty" dealbreaker in Q2 (top-ranked in the aggregator list).

### Gaps
- No study found on bitter-about-ex bios or negativity; no platform data specific to 40+ men.

## 4. Scam, catfishing and fake-profile fears; protective behaviours

### Takeaway
Romance-scam losses are large, but sources conflict and none gave 40+ male breakdowns.

### Cited Findings
- FTC: 55,604 romance-scam complaints Jan-Sep 2025, over $1.16 billion reported losses [MEDIUM] — [Global Dating Insights](https://globaldatinginsights.com/2026/02/26/romance-scams-outpace-other-fraud-types-at-1-16-billion/)
- FBI IC3 2024: 17,910 romance complaints, $672 million [MEDIUM] — [WFTV](https://www.wftv.com/news/romance-scams-hearts-broken-wallets-emptied/VYAQGLOAF5NCPFCCEKEEAKB3M4/)
- Older-adult loss figures conflict ($2.4bn vs $4.9bn vs ~$700m) because they measure different things [MEDIUM] — [Review42](https://resources.review42.com/catfishing-statistics/); [PlanAdviser](https://www.planadviser.com/scams-targeting-seniors-soaring/)
- Claim that men account for 73% of romance-scam losses: single news source, unverified against FTC [ANECDOTAL] — [WSB-TV](https://www.wsbtv.com/news/romance-scams-hearts-broken-wallets-emptied/VYAQGLOAF5NCPFCCEKEEAKB3M4/)
- Widowed, divorced or isolated people appear at higher risk; scam profiles often unusually appealing — same WSB-TV source [ANECDOTAL]

### Gaps
- No 40-69 male breakdown (FTC Consumer Sentinel explorer is the place to check); no survey on 40+ men's fear of scams; protective behaviours (video call early, reverse image search, never send money) not evidenced in my searches.
