# Conversation psychology for daters 35+ (USA, Canada, UK): evidence file for WOLFE

Audience: adults about 35+ dating again after divorce, separation, widowhood, a long relationship, or a long single spell.
Method note: this is a search-snippet-based review. WebFetch was not used for primary papers and Reddit was not used. Most facts below were read in search-result summaries, not in the full paper or report. Where a number appears, the snippet it came from is named. Anything I could not see is marked "not verified".

Evidence labels: **strong** = meta-analysis, large representative survey, replicated peer-reviewed. **medium** = single solid study, reputable survey, dated source. **anecdotal** = coaching blogs, opinion. **inference** = my reasoning, not a finding.

Bottom line first: there is very little research on first conversations among daters 35+. Most conversation experiments used students or strangers in labs. The "why it is harder" list for 35+ is mostly plausible inference plus coaching opinion. A product can say that honestly.

### What is plausible inference or coaching opinion (not directly measured in 35+ daters)

I found no study I could verify that measures each item below in 35+ first-time-in-years daters. Treat all as inference unless noted.

- **Rusty social skills.** Plausible: less recent practice, new platform norms. No measured data found. Label: inference.
- **Guardedness, baggage, trust issues.** Coaching and advice blogs describe divorce leaving people doubting others and second-guessing a date's motives (e.g. Regal Beagle, https://theregalbeagle.beehiiv.com/p/dating-divorce-difficult). Label: anecdotal.
- **Fear of rejection and looking foolish.** Same sources and The Modest Man (https://www.themodestman.com/hidden-dating-fears-men-over-40/) describe fear of being hurt again; some men avoid dating to protect themselves. Label: anecdotal. Related lab evidence on "liking gap" (section 3) suggests people also underestimate how much others like them, so some fear is likely miscalibrated. That link to 35+ daters is inference.
- **Comparing with the ex or late spouse.** Several advice pages (funeral.com, marriage.com) warn against measuring a new person against a former spouse and say wanting a "replica" may signal not being ready. Label: anecdotal.
- **Time scarcity (work, kids).** Very plausible and widely repeated; I did not find a source I could verify that measures it for dating conversations. Label: inference.
- **Small-talk fatigue.** I found no verified research using this term. Label: inference. Do not present as a finding.
- **Negative self-talk about age/body.** Advice blogs mention age stereotypes and self-doubt (e.g. https://vocal.media/confessions/thriving-in-relationships-after-40). Label: anecdotal.
- **Grief and guilt after divorce or widowhood.** Widowhood advice sources describe guilt, rawness in early bereavement, and no universal "right time" (funeral.com; tawkify; pubtic; links in section 5). Label: anecdotal. The 2019 gray-divorce study above is peer-reviewed on depressive symptoms and recovery but does not study dating conversation.

## 2. What men and women 35+ tend to find hard to say or ask

Honest caveat: I found no study that asks 35+ men and women "what is hardest to say on a first date." What follows combines survey data on online-dating messaging (US, all ages unless stated) with inference. Do not write gendered claims in a product as "research shows" unless they are from the Pew rows.

### How men and women experience online-dating messaging (US)

Source for the numbers: a news summary of Pew's 2023 report (Business Report, https://busrep.co.za/lifestyle/love-sex/relationships/finding-love-sex-and-harassment-on-dating-apps-e3a81ec8-9a5c-4152-b8c5-4b578caedf55), which the search tool returned; the underlying Pew report is "From Looking for Love to Swiping the Field" and "The Experiences of U.S. Online Daters" (Feb 2, 2023, https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2023/02/02/the-experiences-of-u-s-online-daters/). I did not see the Pew table directly, so the figures below should be rechecked on pewresearch.org before printing.

| Experience (US online daters, all adult ages) | Men | Women | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feel they do not get enough messages | 57% | 24% | medium to strong; secondary read |
| Feel they get too many messages | 8% | 30% | same |
| Received unsolicited sexual message/image (reported in summary as 46% of women; a different figure, 38% of all online daters, was cited elsewhere) | n/a in snippet | 46% (disputed across sources) | unclear, do not quote |

The sources disagree on the harassment numbers (46%, 38%, and a Statista 2022 figure of 56% for women 18-49 vs 25% for men). Because of that conflict I recommend quoting only the direction: women are much more likely than men to report harassment and unwanted explicit contact, and the gap is larger under 50. Pew 2020 (primary page seen in results): 60% of women under 35 who online dated said someone kept contacting them after they said no, versus 27% of men under 35. That is under-35s, so it is not a 35+ figure.

### Likely difficulties by gender (mostly inference, with data pointers)

**Men 35+**
- Getting replies at all. Pew shows men are far more likely to feel they receive too few messages (57% vs 24%, see above). Label: medium (data) for "men feel they get too few messages"; the link to what they find hard to say is inference.
- Asking directly for a date without sounding pushy, and showing interest without pressure. Inference.
- Talking about feelings, divorce, or fears. Coaching blogs say some men avoid dating to protect themselves from rejection (The Modest Man, https://www.themodestman.com/?p=751231). Label: anecdotal.
- Gray-divorce research: repartnering especially reduces loneliness for men (Journals of Gerontology B 2020). Label: medium. This is about outcomes, not conversation.

**Women 35+**
- Saying "no" or "not interested" clearly and safely, because persistent contact after refusal is common for younger women (Pew 2020). For 35+ women the gap is not broken out in what I saw. Label: medium for under-35s; inference for 35-49.
- Handling a high volume of messages (30% of women feel they get too many, Pew 2023 via secondary summary).
- Stating what they want, including pace and exclusivity, without being labelled "too much." Inference.
- Women are more likely to avoid repartnering after gray divorce (study above, 50+, US). Label: medium. Reasons are not conversation-specific.

Both: asking about intentions, saying "I'm nervous," saying "I haven't done this in years," and ending politely. Inference.

## 3. What conversation behaviours research supports

All items below come from studies mostly of students, strangers, or general adults, not 35+ daters. Say so in the product.

### 3.1 Ask questions, especially follow-ups
- **Finding:** Huang, Yeomans, Brooks, Minson and Gino (2017), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, "It doesn't hurt to ask: Question-asking increases liking." Across studies, people who asked more questions, particularly follow-ups, were better liked. In a speed-dating dataset, people who asked more follow-up questions were more likely to get a second-date "yes" from the partner. People did not anticipate this effect.
- **Sources:** https://www.hks.harvard.edu/centers/cpl/publications/it-doesnt-hurt-ask-question-asking-increases-liking ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6857721/
- **Dispute:** Kluger and Malloy published a critique arguing the speed-dating data should be analysed with a social relations model, that agreeing to a second date is not the same as liking, and that asking is not the same as listening. The original authors replied in 2019 (JPSP) and stand by the conclusion; a later correction notice says an audit found minor reporting errors but that all conclusions are valid (per the search summaries; I did not read the papers). So this is a **medium** finding with a published methods dispute, not a settled one.
- **Limits:** Speed-dating participants were a small academic sample (US-based, not 35+-specific); I could not verify the sample ages from the snippets. Follow-up question counts in the speed-dating study came from an automated detector.

### 3.2 The liking gap
- **Finding:** Boothby, Cooney, Sandstrom and Clark (2018), Psychological Science. After conversations, people underestimate how much their partners liked them and enjoyed the conversation. Seen with lab strangers, first-year dorm mates (persisting for months), and members of the public at a workshop. Observers could judge the liking accurately. The authors link it to people being absorbed in worry about what they said.
- **Source:** https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/The-Liking-Gap-in-Conversations.pdf (found in results; read through summaries only).
- **Label:** medium to strong (several studies; I did not check replications).
- **Limits:** Not romantic first dates specifically as far as the summaries show; not 35+. A safe use: "Many people leave a conversation thinking it went worse than the other person felt."

### 3.3 Escalating, reciprocal self-disclosure
- **Finding:** Aron, Melinat, Aron, Vallone and Bator (1997), Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin 23(4): pairs of unacquainted people who took turns answering 36 increasingly personal questions reported more closeness than pairs given small-talk questions. The authors themselves said it is unlikely to produce loyalty, dependence or commitment, which take longer.
- **Sources:** https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/36_questions_for_increasing_closeness ; summaries at PsychCentral and Futility Closet.
- **Label:** medium (single-lab originating study; summaries only).
- **Limits:** Structured lab task, mostly students, pairs instructed to do it. It is not proof that this feels right on a first date, and the search did not return evidence on when disclosure backfires. Do not claim "36 questions make people fall in love."
- **Practical reading (inference):** match the other person's depth; do not skip ahead.

### 3.4 Turn-taking
- I did not find a source I could verify on turn-taking for dating conversation. Label: no verified evidence. Common-sense advice (let the other person finish, share roughly equally) can be given as coaching opinion, not research.

## 4. Who should ask whom: first moves and initiation

Evidence is thin, old, and mostly platform-specific.

| Claim | Source | Label | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| On OkCupid, men sent a median of about 9-15 first messages versus 3-4 for women (varied by age); women who messaged first were about 2.5 times more likely to get a reply than men who did. | OkCupid data via NYT/CNN-style coverage, 2016 (e.g. https://amp.cnn.com/cnn/2016/03/14/living/okcupid-womens-research-feat ; https://jezebel.com/women-who-message-dudes-first-on-okcupid-are-getting-mo-1763848001) | anecdotal to medium (company data, dated, not peer-reviewed as seen) | One platform, 2015-2016, heterosexual focus, not age-specific. |

## 5. Sensitive topics and how to handle them honestly

Important: I did not find direct Gottman, APA, BACP or CPA guidance on these topics in the searches (a Gottman-specific search returned nothing relevant). What follows is from advice pages and a few academic sources, so most is **anecdotal**. Where I describe an "early vs later" split, it is my synthesis of that advice and common counselling practice, not a researched rule. Do not attribute it to a professional body.

| Topic | What advice sources say | Label | Suggested honest stance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ex-partner | Talking mostly about an ex on a first date can signal not being ready (iBelieve, https://www.ibelieve.com/relationships/dating-after-forty-how-to-avoid-common-pitfalls.html). Comparing a date to an ex or late spouse is discouraged. | anecdotal | A brief, neutral fact ("I was married for 14 years; it ended two years ago") is fine; save details for later. |
| Kids | Sources say be cautious about introducing kids to new partners (OurFamilyWizard; one Christian advice column suggests waiting a year or more). An academic chapter on divorced mothers found children's rapport with a partner was associated with later breakup. Dated and narrow. | anecdotal; one chapter medium | Mention that you have kids early (it affects availability); introduce them later, once things are steady. Inference. |
| Divorce | Healing before dating is a repeated theme. | anecdotal | Say it is in the past; do not litigate it. |
| Widowhood | Be honest but measured about a late spouse, then move to shared interests (funeral.com). Timing varies widely. Dwelling on the loss throughout can strain a new relationship (marriage.com). | anecdotal | It is fine to say "I'm widowed" early; grief can come up and does not need hiding. |
| Age | Age stereotypes cause self-doubt (blogs). | anecdotal | Be honest about age in profile and chat. Do not lie. |
| Work/money | Not found in verified sources beyond cost-of-dating surveys. | no evidence | Light mention early; detail later. Inference. |
| Health | Not found. | no evidence | Share what affects plans when it becomes relevant. Inference. |
| "What are you looking for?" | Not found in verified sources. | no evidence | Plain early question; reasonable to ask by the first few dates. Inference. |
| Exclusivity | Not found. | no evidence | Raise it directly when it starts to matter, not by guessing. Inference. |
| Intimacy / pace | Not found. | no evidence | Say your pace out loud; consent and comfort matter. Inference. |

Other finding to use carefully: recovering from loss varies. A 2019 gray-divorce study found divorced people recovered from elevated depressive symptoms faster than the widowed (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6565490). That is population-level, 50+, US; do not apply it to individuals.

## 7. Do not use list (stats and claims I could not verify)

- Any "X% of people over 40 are lonely/afraid/ghosted/unable to date" figure. I found no source.
- "Divorced people over 40 date X times before remarriage," "X% of second marriages fail" or similar. Not verified in this review.
- "It takes N months/years to be ready after divorce/widowhood." Sources say timing varies widely; no research number found.
- "Men 35+ send X messages for every reply" or "women 35+ receive X messages a day." Not found. The only message-volume figures I saw are Pew's all-age numbers via a secondary summary.
- The harassment percentages for women: figures conflicted (46%, 38%, 56%, 60% among under-35). Use direction only.
- "36 questions make you fall in love" and "second date is guaranteed if you ask follow-up questions." Overstates Aron 1997 and Huang 2017.
- "People decide in seven seconds / 3 minutes whether they like you." Not verified.
- "Women who message first get 2.5x more replies" as a current fact. It is 2016 OkCupid data from one platform.
- "Most Canadians use X app" or "UK men pay on first dates." Not found / unverified.
- Gottman, APA, BACP or CPA rulings on dating after divorce or widowhood. I did not find any; do not name them as backing.
- The Jasmine Directory and similar low-quality pages reproduced Pew numbers I could not confirm.
